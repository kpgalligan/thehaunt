# The Road-Strip Cast

Invented under Kevin's 2026-08-27 commission ("use your best creative energy and fill
in the blanks"), building on `README.md`'s canon. Everything here is proposed canon:
names, voices, and details are Kevin's to keep, rename, or strike. Canon-named
characters (Billie, Bud, Sam, Abe) keep their names; everyone else's name is new.

The tone law governing every line of dialogue: Act I is entirely cozy, and dread
arrives only through dialogue, only at the "look twice and move on" grade. The town's
open secret shapes every conversation — before the town-hall meeting, every local
knows something Jane doesn't, so their kindness has pity in it; after `meeting_done`,
the masks can slip half an inch. Nobody mentions the pit. Nobody explains the drug
trade, who manages the sacrifices, Bud's war, or Abe's full story — those are all
deferred reveals, and the dialogue walks up to them and stops.

## West Entry — the strangers' frame

### Walt (motel proprietor) — INVENTED NAME
Canon: middle-aged alcoholic, gruff, steeped in rumor, unreliable but partly useful;
quiet mornings, insightful 2–5 PM, maudlin after.
Invented: he lights two lamps of an evening — the lobby and the one paying room
(the motel is the handoff's four-room motor court). His wife June died eleven years
ago — "lost" is all he ever says. The registry is the one thing he keeps precise.
His 2–5 window is the town's best rumor tap, and the game models it literally: his
dialogue changes with the clock (grunts before 2 PM, a question hub 2–5, June and
the ledger after). Voice: sandpaper declaratives; the fourth drink makes him accurate
before it makes him sad.

### Dennis (gas station clerk) — INVENTED NAME
Canon: 20-something incel, sarcastic, rarely insightful; the station sells food now,
mission-useful pills with side effects later.
Invented: pathetic-funny, never menacing. He is the only person in town who finds the
town weird in mundane ways — the hand-crank pumps, the missing signal, "nobody else
seems bothered" — which makes him accidentally the player's surrogate. He will
complain forever and never leave. Voice: deadpan retail despair.

### Gloria (fireworks stand) — INVENTED NAME
Canon: oddly progressive; pre-Roe campus activist; came to town to pick up a large
amount of cannabis, got married while doing mushrooms; he died years ago; sells
fireworks to get by.
Invented: 74, silver braid, radical-grandma warmth; calls everyone "honey" and means
it; arrived in '74 "to pick something up for a friend" (the drug trade winked at,
never named). She is entirely unafraid of the town — which should itself read as
strange — and the one adult who talks to Jane like an equal from day one. Voice: long
warm sentences with one flint edge. Her husband's name, Otis, is also invented.

### Mr. Pell (motel guest) — WHOLLY INVENTED CHARACTER
Grounded in canon's "the motel is where many of the sacrifices will ultimately come
from" and "the town is much more likely to make the demand when strangers arrive and
stay." A notions salesman three weeks into a one-night stay, unnervingly content:
sleeps deeper than he has since boyhood, keeps meaning to leave, finds it "a kind of
mercy" that leaving stops seeming important. He is the west end's single Act-I dread
tell, and he is never threatened on screen. Walt almost says something about him,
once, and doesn't. Voice: soft, courteous, zero complaints.

## Billie's — the locals' frame

### Billie (canon name)
Limp, eye patch, beaten man, future ally. Early game he lets Jane know she's barely
welcome — but the hostility has one inch of decency in it (the stool by the door is
nobody's). After the meeting: "your money's the same color as anyone's." His
curse-fighting past stays entirely unspoken. Voice: economy; serves without looking.

### Bud (canon name)
The end of the bar, open to close, every shift. Old, oblique, never names the war or
the town's thing; occasionally drops the truest sentence in the room and goes back to
his glass ("Either way, the town gets its way."). Deeper story deferred.

### The shifts (all patron names INVENTED)
Canon says "some leave and are replaced" — so the shifts blur at their edges rather
than swapping on the hour: Pete lingers past Moody, Lyle turns up before three, Ray
gets in a little ahead of Nora.
- **Morning (~10 AM–3 PM): Pete & Moody.** Pete — retired postman, does the crossword
  aloud, wrong ("Seven letters, 'homeward.' Doesn't fit. Never fits."). Moody — a
  nickname nobody remembers the origin of; friendliest man in town before noon, gone
  by three.
- **Afternoon (~3–8 PM): Lyle & Harriet.** Lyle — odd-jobs man, conspiracy-minded
  about mundane things only (mail times, well water), never the real secret. Harriet
  — sharp ex-schoolteacher, gin, taught half the town; the one patron who says Jane's
  situation out loud before the meeting ("Oh, child. You BOUGHT?").
- **Evening (~8 PM–close): Ray & Nora.** Ray — quiet construction-crew drinker
  ("First one's for my back. Second one's for the quiet."). Nora — an ordinary local
  who comes for the company; the cozy anchor who invites Jane to potlucks and the
  harvest dance. Normal life, insisting on itself.

## East Entry

### Sam (canon name)
The salon. Gender unclear — the writing uses no pronouns for Sam, ever. Speaks in
vaguely poetic statements that mostly rhyme; the lines that don't rhyme are the ones
that land ("Scissors know what the season knows: everything grows back. Almost
everything grows."). Haircuts exist mechanically later; early game Sam is texture.
The salon has a checkerboard floor and a stained-glass piece nobody asks about.

### Police station / hardware store
Both deferred per canon. No NPCs cast; the hardware sign stays "closed until further
notice" (the owner's hospital stay is mentioned by no one — the sign doesn't say it).

## East Fork

### Abe (canon name)
The shack. Gentle, precise, slightly formal; twenty years here and content. He never
gossips and never elaborates; his one sharp edge is the distinction that rules his
life: "I never bought so much as a fence post." After the meeting he offers Jane the
thing only he can offer — errands beyond the town line, because he can leave. His
fuller story stays deferred.

## Mechanical notes (for future writing)
- Dialogue variety is selector-driven: Walt varies by time of day, Gloria/Billie/
  Harriet/Abe by `meeting_done`, Sam/Bud/Dennis alternate by day parity.
- All new NPCs are ambient (no beats, no flags raised). Keepers stand on open floor
  beside their counters, so the Talk prompt never depends on the probe reaching over
  furniture.
- Interiors invented to house the cast: the bar room (hearth nook, sealed back bar),
  the motel lobby (two windows: one lit, one dark), the salon (checker floor), the
  gas station shop (one empty shelf). 2026-08-27, from the motel/signage handoff: the
  motel is now the drawn four-room motor court — rooms 1/2/4 share a shell, room 3 is
  Pell's (lit window; the radio behind the locked door is heard,
  not yet drawn — the furniture atlas has no radio piece), all four locked in Act I.
  The other road buildings still wear placeholder faces, now with their sign mounts.
- The drive-in (docs/story/README.md) has no cast yet: it is shut, boarded, and
  marquee-dead ("CLO ED"). Jane's refurbishment goal will need a story seam later.
