The Haunt · Motel & Town Signage · Art Kit v3 · mockup pass

A motel that was new in 1958
and has been open ever since

Late-50s googie bones, maintained but tired: porcelain enamel panels that have chalked, a repainted stripe, one dead bulb on the sign rail. Same 16px grid and same palette as the town and farm kits, plus the two reserved palette slots finally spent — on neon.

One blocker. docs/story/README.md and docs/story/cast.md aren't on main — nothing under docs/story/ exists, and no file in the repo mentions a motel. So this pass is the motel plus the signage system. The per-location signs (which stores, which bars, the police station's name) wait on that push.

01 · The lot, by day

26 × 18 tiles. Office on the left with the upswept eave, a straight run of four rooms to the right, asphalt apron between them and the dirt road. Every room opens onto the parking lot — no corridor, no shared entrance. The player can walk up to all four doors from the moment they find the place.

02 · The lot, at night

The sign is the brightest object in the game. Lobby lit, one room lit, three dark — the occupancy tell reads before any dialogue does. Which room is lit should be a story flag, not decoration.

THE READ
Cream enamel, aqua stripe, alternating red and aqua doors. Only the sign is saturated. The building itself is the palest mass in the town — it should feel bleached, not colourful.
TIRED, NOT DERELICT
Chalking speckle on the enamel, cracks in the asphalt, faded stall stripes, wear ruts in the road. Nothing broken, nothing boarded. Somebody sweeps here.
THE ICE MACHINE
End-of-strip alcove with ice and two vending machines. Cheap ambient light source at night, and a natural place to hide an item or a note.

Locked feedback. A locked door shouldn't fail silently. Same Door node with an IsEnabled predicate; when it's false the interaction prompt still fires and returns a line. Rattling a locked handle four times in a row is a cheap, effective way to make the player feel watched.

04 · The vacancy sign

Neon is glass, and glass is bent by hand and paid for by the letter. Nobody buys two panels. The sign carries NO VACANCY in tube from the day it was installed, and the NO simply doesn't get switched on — which is nearly every night.

The V has a bad transformer and drops out for about half a second every four. It is never remarked on and never fixed, because fixing it costs money the motel doesn't have. That's the point of it.

Two circuits, one panel: NO on its own, VACANCY on another, and the V wired off the second through the failing transformer. The nameplate between MOTEL and the vacancy box is deliberately blank — one ruled line where the motel's name goes. Fill it once the story docs land.

05 · Four ways a sign attaches

The mounting type does the era work, so the lettering can stay plain. Top row day, bottom row night. Every business in town uses exactly one of these four.

POLE
Set back from the road, meant to be read from a car. Motel, and anything else that expects passing traffic. The most expensive-looking mount in town.
WALL BAND
Flush letters on a dark band above the door. Civic and institutional: police station, clinic, anything the town paid for. Lit from below.
HANGING BRACKET
Perpendicular to the facade on an iron arm, one bulb over it. Bars and anything on a walkable street — it's the only mount readable side-on.
WINDOW
Band sign plus a small neon word in the glass. Stores. The neon word is the open/closed state, so it doubles as a shop-hours tell.

06 · Rules for every sign in town

One 3×5 pixel alphabet. Every sign in the game uses the same 3×5 glyph set, at 1× for small signs and 2× for the motel's MOTEL panel. No second typeface. It keeps signs readable at 480×270 and makes new signs nearly free to author.
Two lit colours only. Neon red #e05a3f and aqua #5fb9b0 — the two reserved palette slots. Incandescent bulbs use the existing lamp amber. Nothing else glows.
Day and night are two sprites, not a tint. Unlit day, lit night, same footprint. Swap by time of day; the light pool underneath is a separate additive sprite so it can be reused.
City hall gets nothing. Confirmed — no exterior sign. It reads as civic because it's the only masonry facade, which is stronger than lettering. Civic signalling comes in a later pass.
Signs are where dread lands. A sign is a legible object with a fixed shape, so a single changed letter is unmissable. Later acts should swap sprites, not add new ones — one letter dead, a word repainted, the vacancy panel showing something it shouldn't.
Nothing animates but the V. One flickering element in the whole town. The moment a second thing flickers, the first stops meaning anything.
