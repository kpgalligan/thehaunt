01 · Proposed farm layout

The real 40 × 30 grid from TestMap.cs, at 640 × 480. Every tile here is a real asset — this is a layout proposal, not a painting.

THE READ
House upper-left, barn upper-right, a single track between them running east to the town road. Plots sit below the track so the player crosses their own work every time they leave.
WHY TWO PLOTS
One dry, one watered, so the soil-state difference is legible on sight from anywhere on the farm. The dry plot is the bigger one — most of a farm is waiting.
THE EXIT
Road leaves east at rows 13–15 and the boundary is woods on all four sides. Same diegetic rule as town: the map limit is forest, never a wall.

02 · Soil, with edges

Today FarmSoil is two flat tiles, so a plot reads as a rectangle of mud. This adds a 16-config autotile for dry and wet, plus a furrow set that shows the hoe's direction.

ROW 0
2 dry soil, 2 wet soil, 4 furrow (dry/wet × horizontal/vertical), 2 pasture, 2 weeds, 2 rocks, stump, puddle
ROW 1
Dry soil over grass, 16 configs. Same naming as the town dirt autotile, so one bitmask helper serves both.
ROW 2
Wet soil over grass, 16 configs. Watering a plot edge should change its silhouette, not just its color.
ROW 3
Fence rails, post, 4 corners, gate open/closed, 2 path, pasture, dead grass, flowers, log, hay scatter

Furrows are a cheap, high-value tell. A tile hoed north–south looks different from one hoed east–west, so a plot the player worked in rows reads as deliberate rather than sprayed. It needs one extra byte on the tile record, or it can be derived from neighbours at paint time.

03 · Crops

Four crops, five columns each: stages 0–3 plus the mature column, exactly matching StageForDay. Row order follows CropDefs — turnip, green bean, potato, cauliflower.

STAGE 0 → MATURE, LEFT TO RIGHT
Row Crop Height Mature silhouette
0turnipin tileLeaf rosette with a plum shoulder breaking the soil
1greenbeanoverhangsTwin stakes, dense vine, six hanging pods. Tallest thing on the farm.
2potatoin tileBroad low mound, five cream flowers, disturbed soil at the base
3caulifloweroverhangsWide dark leaves cradling a cream head. The premium crop should look expensive.

Cells are 16 × 32, not 16 × 16. Short crops sit in the lower half; beans and cauliflower use the overhang. In Godot, set the Crops atlas texture_region_size to (16, 32) and texture_origin to (0, -8). The cell-state function in the phase 2 spec is unchanged: row of CropId, column StageForDay(GrowthDay).

The Crops layer must draw above FarmSoil and below Obstacles — the existing order already does this. One caveat with overhang: a tall crop drawn on row Y overlaps row Y−1, so the layer needs its own Y-sort or the plot should never sit directly below something the player walks behind.

04 · The barn, in three states

Nothing in the codebase mentions a barn, so this is a proposal as much as an asset. It came with the farm, it is falling down, and the player fixes it. Board-and-batten siding, gambrel roof, 6 × 7 tiles with the bottom 5 rows as footprint.

STATE 0Derelict
Holes in the roof, missing boards, one collapsed corner, a single door hanging. Interior visible as darkness through every gap. Weeds at the base. Windows are black — no glass left.
STATE 1Weathertight
Structurally sound and visibly unfinished: fresh pale patches on the roof, new doors hung, glass in the windows, unpainted siding. This is the state that should last longest.
STATE 2Restored
Barn red with cream trim, slate roof, cupola vent, lit windows. The only saturated red mass in the game — which makes finishing it the most visible thing the player has ever done.

Two design notes. Repair should be a story flag, not a percentage — three states means three clean reads, and a slider would need art for every value. And the derelict barn is a natural home for an Act I dread tell: it is the one structure the player is invited to look at closely, which makes it the cheapest place to hide something that was always there.

05 · The interior system

Two atlases furnish every room in the game. Floors are deliberately quiet — low contrast, staggered joints — so furniture and characters carry the read. Walls are the loud surface.

INTERIOR.PNG — ROW 0 FLOORS · ROW 1 WALL LOWER + OPENINGS · ROW 2 WALL UPPER, CEILING, RAFTERS · ROW 3 HEARTH, COUNTER, SHELVES, STORAGE
FURNITURE.PNG — 16×32 UPRIGHTS · 16×16 SMALLS · 32×16 SURFACES · LARGER PIECES BELOW

The wall system is a single-thickness ring, matching FarmHouseMap exactly — no collision changes. The north wall uses a row-2 tile (dark band at top, reads as ceiling shadow); east, west and south use row 1. Windows and doors are drop-in replacements for a wall tile at the same coordinate.

06 · Six rooms

Three exist in code and are drawn at their real dimensions with furniture on the real coordinates. Three are proposals, with a suggested size.

FARMHOUSE · 14 × 10 · EXISTS
Log walls, plank floor. Bed at (12,2)–(12,3), chest at (2,2), table at (6,4)–(7,4), door at (7,9) — every position matches FarmHouseMap.cs. Stove and hearth added; the hearth is what makes an interior feel warmer than outside at dusk.
GENERAL STORE · 14 × 10 · EXISTS
Wainscot walls, counter across the middle with the till, seed bins on the shelf wall. The four shelf colors map to the four seed packets on the ratified shelf — turnip, potato, green bean, cauliflower — so stock is readable before the shop UI opens.
BARN INTERIOR · 16 × 12 · PROPOSED
Dirt floor, hay scatter, hayloft edge along the north wall with a ladder up to it. Two stalls, workbench, tool rack, cart, crates. Drawn in the derelict state — cobwebs in the corners, stains on the floor. The restored version swaps the floor stains out and lights the lantern.
MEETING HOUSE · 16 × 14 · PROPOSED
Plaster walls, board floor, a runner up the aisle, six pews, altar under two stained windows. The stained glass is the only place plum and lantern sit side by side — worth reserving for exactly one room, and worth remembering when the town's secrets need somewhere to have been kept.
NEIGHBOR HOME · 12 × 9 · PROPOSED
Deliberately smaller than the player's farmhouse. Bed, cradle, hearth, loom, one shuttered window. A template: change the furniture, keep the shell, and every resident gets a home for the cost of a layout function.
TOWN HALL · 40 × 23 · EXISTS
The largest interior in the game by a wide margin, and the only one that needs stone. Checkered floor, stone walls, a long table on a runner, eight pews, clerks' desks at both ends, banners flanking the lectern. Shown below at full width.

At 40 × 23 the hall is wider than the 30 × 17 viewport, so the player never sees all of it at once. That is worth keeping — it is the only interior with anything to walk toward.

07 · Integration

FarmSoil goes from 2 tiles to 36: rows 1 and 2 of farm_terrain.png are the dry and wet autotile sets, and the cell-state function stays exactly as specified — none / dry / wet, with the config picked from the four neighbours.

Crops keeps its one-row-per-CropDef layout; only the cell size changes, to 16 × 32 with an 8px upward origin.

Interiors need no structural change. Swap the four procedural colors for atlas coordinates and keep the single-thickness ring, the near-black Surround ColorRect, and the Door-in-the-south-wall convention.

Barn needs a map, a footprint on the farm, a Door, and one story flag with three values. The interior can ship as a single state first; the exterior states are the part worth having early, because they are what the player sees from the yard every day.
